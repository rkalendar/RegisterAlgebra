Register Algebra · interactive demonstration

Every number here is two integers, read as 2a + 3b.

An integer is a pair of arbitrary-precision registers. A rational is a pair of pairs whose denominator has non-zero value; the quotient of numerator by denominator is never formed. This page runs the paper's algorithms in your browser. All arithmetic is done on BigInt; floating point is used only to draw the chart.

At the two marked stations the registers are evaluated to integers and divided exactly by their greatest common divisor; halving then restores the canonical form, as it does when the operands are encoded. These are the integer divisions of the system. What never happens is the division of a numerator by its denominator.

Encode an integer

§4 canonical form

Canonicalisation sends every integer to the unique pair whose second register is 0 or 1: even numbers to (N/2, 0), odd numbers to ((N−3)/2, 1). Equivalently, if N = 2q + r with r in {0, 1}, the pair is (q − r, r), so the canonical form holds the quotient and the parity of N in other coordinates and nothing more. Try a negative number, or something with two hundred digits.

Compute with fractions

§8 algorithms 1–3

Every intermediate value below is a register pair. The counter tallies integer operations as Table 5 of the paper counts them: seven multiplications and two additions for each register product. These are counts in the unit-cost model, not running times. The registers are arbitrary-precision integers, so an addition costs time linear in their length and a product costs the time of a big-integer multiplication.

The representative matters

§5.3 normalisation

The map (a, b) ↦ 2a + 3b is not injective: (a + 3k, b − 2k) represents the same integer for every k. In particular a pair can represent zero without being (0, 0), which is why a denominator must have non-zero value. Enter any pair to see its value and its canonical representative, obtained in register space as (a + 3⌊b/2⌋, b mod 2) without evaluating the pair.

Formula (5) returns a raw pair that is in general not canonical, even for canonical inputs, and its first register is 2ac whatever the values are. Squaring the canonical pair for 1 again and again shows the consequence: every pair in the chain still represents 1, but its registers double in length at every step. Normalising after each product keeps the pair at (−1, 1).

    Register Algebra is an alternative encoding of ordinary exact arithmetic, not a new number system and not a fast arithmetic: in canonical form the pair carries the same information as ⌊N/2⌋ and N mod 2, and no workload measured in the paper shows an advantage. This page demonstrates the formalism. For production exact arithmetic use GMP or your language's rational type.
