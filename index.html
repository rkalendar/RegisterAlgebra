Register Algebra · interactive demonstration

Every number here is two integers, read as 2a + 3b.

An integer is a register pair. A rational is a pair of pairs, kept as the image of a division that is never carried out. This page runs the paper's algorithms in your browser on BigInt only — no floating-point number is created at any point.

Division appears exactly once in the whole computation — at the marked station, where the greatest common divisor cancels the fraction. Everything before it is addition and multiplication of integers.

Encode an integer

§3 canonical form

Canonicalisation sends every integer to the unique pair whose second register is 0 or 1: even numbers to (N/2, 0), odd numbers to ((N−3)/2, 1). Try a negative number, or something with two hundred digits.

Why reduction is not optional

§11.3 growth

Formulas (7a)–(7d) stay exact without ever reducing, but the registers grow. Both curves below compute the same harmonic sum 1 + 1/2 + … + 1/n; one reduces after every operation, the other does not.
